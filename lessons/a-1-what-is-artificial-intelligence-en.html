What Is Artificial Intelligence?

A practical introduction to AI: definitions, intelligent behaviour, system architecture, major approaches and limitations.

Learning outcomes
  • Define the key concepts accurately
  • Distinguish methods and system components
  • Explain the main workflow using diagrams
  • Identify limitations and describe validation
Approx. 60 minutes

1. AI as an engineering field

Artificial Intelligence is a family of methods and systems designed to perform tasks associated with intelligent capabilities: perception, language, reasoning, planning, learning and decision support. There is no single definition accepted for every discipline. In engineering, describe the task, operating conditions and measurable limits rather than asking whether a machine is human-like.

2. Intelligence is not consciousness

A system can classify images, translate text or plan a route without evidence of subjective experience. Intelligent behaviour is observable task performance. Consciousness concerns subjective experience and is a separate scientific and philosophical question. Task success alone does not prove consciousness.

3. The complete AI system

A deployed product includes data acquisition, validation, preprocessing, a model or rule engine, postprocessing, an interface, monitoring and sometimes human review. A model is only one component of this pipeline.

4. Major approaches

Rule-based systems apply explicit rules. Search and planning explore possible states and actions. Machine learning estimates patterns from examples. Hybrid systems combine methods. A useful AI system does not necessarily use a neural network—or machine learning at all.

5. Capabilities and limits

Performance depends on the task, data, environment and evaluation method. AI can fail on unfamiliar inputs, biased samples, changing conditions and ambiguous requests. Fluent output is not proof of correctness. High-impact applications need traceability, validation and appropriate human oversight.

6. Case study

A building-energy assistant may combine fixed safety rules, a forecasting model and an application. Rules prevent unsafe recommendations; the model estimates consumption; the application checks units and presents evidence. The useful product is the complete workflow.

7. Review

Explain why a rule-based route planner may be AI without using machine learning. Give an example of intelligent behaviour that does not establish consciousness. Name two components surrounding a deployed model.

Review

Define the key terms in your own words, give a real-world example and explain how you would validate the system's output.

Key terminology

Artificial Intelligence (AI)
A broad field concerned with systems that perform tasks associated with intelligent capabilities.
Intelligent behaviour
Observable performance on a task; it does not by itself imply consciousness.
Rule-based system
A system whose decisions follow explicit conditions and rules written by people.
Human oversight
A designed process in which a person reviews, approves or can override consequential system outputs.

Worked example: an energy assistant

Separate the components: a meter provides observations; validation checks units and missing values; a forecasting model estimates demand; a safety rule rejects impossible or unsafe recommendations; the interface explains the result. If the forecast is wrong, inspect the whole pipeline rather than assuming the model is the only possible cause.

How the field developed

Early AI research explored symbolic representations, search and explicit reasoning. Later, statistical learning made it possible to estimate patterns from larger datasets. Deep neural networks then improved performance in areas such as image recognition, speech and language. These waves did not simply replace one another: modern systems often combine rules, search, learned models and human expertise.

Narrow AI and general intelligence

Most deployed systems are designed for a limited task or domain. A system that is excellent at translation is not automatically able to plan a building renovation or diagnose equipment. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is a debated research concept, not a capability that should be inferred from one impressive demonstration.

How should an AI system be evaluated?

Evaluation should match the task and consequences: predictive quality, robustness to changed inputs, fairness across relevant groups, security, latency, cost and the ability to explain or review consequential decisions. No single score captures all these dimensions.
