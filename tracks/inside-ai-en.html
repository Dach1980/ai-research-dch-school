TRACK B · RESEARCH

Inside AI

Investigate transformers, internal representations, circuits, sparse autoencoders and mechanistic interpretability.

Advanced research pathModules 27–39Theory · Experiments · Projects

What you will learn

  • Analyze residual streams, attention heads and MLP blocks.
  • Use probes and lenses to inspect internal representations.
  • Run activation patching, ablations and causal tracing.
  • Discover and validate circuits and algorithmic mechanisms.
  • Study superposition, dictionary learning and SAEs.
  • Apply interpretability methods to AI Safety.

Curriculum

27 · Introduction to Mechanistic InterpretabilityResearch questions, explanatory levels and experimental thinking.
28 · Transformer internals and decompositionResidual stream, attention heads, MLP blocks and information flow.
29 · Probes, lenses and representation analysisLinear probes, logit lens, tuned lens and representation diagnostics.
30 · Causal interventions in neural networksActivation patching, ablation, causal tracing and controls.
31 · Circuit discovery and algorithmic interpretationCircuit hypotheses, attribution graphs and validation.
32 · Features, superposition and dictionary learningPolysemanticity, feature geometry and dictionary-learning methods.
33 · Sparse Autoencoders and modern architecturesSAE training, feature quality, scaling and limitations.
34 · Activation steering and knowledge editingSteering vectors, representation editing and behavioural evaluation.
35 · Interpretability for AI SafetyThreat models, monitoring, model auditing and safety evidence.
36 · Robustness, adversarial examples and explanation attacksRobustness tests, adversarial inputs and attacks on explanations.
37 · Developmental InterpretabilityHow representations and circuits form during training.
38 · Advanced methods and research frontiersOpen problems, new methods and critical reading of research.
39 · Final research projectA reproducible investigation with a research question, methods and evidence.
